EXPERIMENT 01 // CLASSICAL ARCHITECTURE

SIGNED-MAGNITUDE MULTIPLICATION

Operands maintain separate sign bits ($0$ positive, $1$ negative) and unsigned magnitude bits. The magnitude is accumulated using the $E:A:Q$ right-shift chain, while the sign is independently calculated as $A_s \leftarrow Q_s \oplus B_s$.

ARCHITECTURAL SPECIFICATION • MANO CONVENTION FIG. 10-1 • 10-2

Registers: B holds multiplicand magnitude, Q holds multiplier magnitude, A is cleared to 0, E is carry/overflow flip-flop (initially 0), and Sequence Counter SC counts $n$ iterations down to 0. Each step inspects the least significant multiplier bit $Q_n$: if $1$, add $B$ to $A$ via parallel adder; then shift right the composite register chain $E:A:Q$ with 0 entering $E$.

EXPERIMENT 02 // 2’S COMPLEMENT RECODING

BOOTH’S MULTIPLICATION ALGORITHM

Directly multiplies signed 2's complement numbers without separate sign logic. By pairing adjacent multiplier bits $(Q_n, Q_{n+1})$, strings of $1$s are recoded into single additions and subtractions across an arithmetic right shift datapath.

[11] NO ARITHMETIC OP • DIRECT ASHR

ARCHITECTURAL ANALYSIS

COMPARATIVE EVALUATION

Contrasting representation models, register organization, shift mechanisms, cycle efficiency, and circuit complexity.

ARCHITECTURAL COMPARISON MATRIX SIDE-BY-SIDE
DIMENSION SIGNED-MAGNITUDE MULTIPLIER BOOTH’S 2’S COMPLEMENT MULTIPLIER
Number Format Decoupled sign bit (0/1) from absolute magnitude. Negative numbers have identical magnitude bits to positive numbers. Integrated 2’s complement notation. Negative numbers carry implicit negative weight on MSB ($-2^{n-1}$).
Sign Computation Handled independently out-of-band: $A_s \leftarrow Q_s \oplus B_s$. Product sign appended after magnitude calculation. Inherently embedded in 2’s complement algebra. No separate sign flip-flops or XOR gates required.
Register Topology Registers: B, Q, A, E (Carry/Overflow bit), SC. Registers: BR, QR, AC, Qn+1 (1-bit history flip-flop), SC.
Shift Mechanism Logical right shift of $E:A:Q$. New 0 shifted into $E$, previous $E$ into $A$, $A$ into $Q$. Arithmetic right shift (ashr) of $AC:QR:Q_{n+1}$. The sign bit ($AC_0$) is duplicated (sign-extended).
Arithmetic Logic Only Addition ($EA \leftarrow A + B$) and shift. Both Addition ($AC \leftarrow AC + BR$) and Subtraction ($AC \leftarrow AC - BR$ via $\overline{BR}+1$).
Efficiency on 1s Executes an addition for every $1$ bit. In worst case ($11111$), requires $n$ full additions. Recodes continuous strings of $1$s into a single subtraction and addition. Substantially faster on runs of 1s.
Silicon Complexity Unidirectional adder, but requires auxiliary sign multiplexers and formatting logic. Requires adder/subtractor control and flip-flop $Q_{n+1}$, but unifies positive and negative datapath.

REGISTER & HARDWARE GLOSSARY

ACCUMULATOR (A / AC)

A primary register that holds the initial zero and subsequent running partial products during multiplication. In final output, it holds the most significant half of the product.

SEQUENCE COUNTER (SC)

A down-counter initialized to the bit width of the multiplier ($n$). It decrements on each shift cycle until reaching zero, signaling hardware completion.

CARRY / OVERFLOW FLIP-FLOP (E)

In signed-magnitude multiplication, a 1-bit flip-flop holding the carry-out generated by the parallel adder. On each shift right step, the bit in $E$ shifts into the MSB of Accumulator $A$, and $E$ is reset to $0$.

PREVIOUS MULTIPLIER BIT (QN+1)

A 1-bit flip-flop used in Booth’s algorithm, initialized to $0$. It records the bit shifted out of the multiplier ($QR$) during the previous arithmetic right shift, forming the pair $(Q_n, Q_{n+1})$.

ARITHMETIC RIGHT SHIFT (ASHR)

A right shift that preserves the sign of a 2's complement number by copying (replicating) the most significant bit ($AC_0$) into the new MSB rather than inserting a $0$.

LOGICAL RIGHT SHIFT (SHR)

A right shift where a $0$ is shifted into the most significant position. Used for unsigned magnitudes and in the $E:A:Q$ signed-magnitude chain.

BOOTH’S BIT-PAIR RECODING

A technique where adjacent multiplier bits are paired: $01$ triggers an addition, $10$ triggers a subtraction, and $00$ / $11$ skip arithmetic operations, drastically accelerating runs of identical bits.
